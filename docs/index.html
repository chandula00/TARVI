Abstract

Reconstructing cell-fate trajectories from single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) is fundamental to understanding development and disease. RNA velocity infers cellular dynamics from unspliced-to-spliced mRNA ratios, but existing methods share three key limitations: transcription rates are static gene-level constants that ignore cell-state regulation; latent cell time is weakly supervised and poorly calibrated; and locally inferred velocity vectors are often inconsistent with global trajectories, producing reversed or discontinuous flows. We present TARVI, a deep probabilistic framework that extends the VeloVI variational autoencoder to address all three limitations, through cell-specific transcription rates driven by transcription factor–target regulatory priors (ChEA/ENCODE), a supervised residual latent-time head, novel velocity–time consistency and temporal-smoothness losses, and a velocity–pseudotime blending step. Evaluated on 5 datasets against 4 baselines with 8 metrics, TARVI matches the strongest baseline on cross-boundary direction accuracy (CBDir: 0.933 vs. VeloVI’s 0.931) while improving Spearman pseudotime correlation by 126% over VeloVI (0.747 vs. 0.331), recovering coherent directional flows and smooth developmental gradients where competing methods fail.

Method

TARVI builds on the VeloVI four-state generative backbone (induction, induction-steady, repression, repression-steady) with an amortised variational encoder, and adds four components that target the limitations above. The biophysical basis is the per-gene splicing ODE system

$$\frac{du_g}{dt}=\alpha_g(t)-\beta_g u_g,\qquad \frac{ds_g}{dt}=\beta_g u_g-\gamma_g s_g,\qquad v_g=\beta_g u_g-\gamma_g s_g,$$

with transcription rate $\alpha_g$, splicing rate $\beta_g$, and degradation rate $\gamma_g$; the RNA velocity is $v_g$.

1 · TF-regulated rate

A masked, learnable TF→gene matrix turns the static $\alpha_g$ into a cell-specific $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i$ from ChEA/ENCODE priors.

2 · Supervised time head

A residual MLP on the latent code yields a calibrated $t_i\in[0,1]$, supervised by a stop-gradient ODE-time signal.

3 · Consistency losses

A velocity–time hinge plus a graph-Laplacian temporal-smoothness term keep the field temporally coherent.

4 · VPT blending

Diffusion pseudotime on the velocity graph is blended with the learned time for a globally grounded coordinate.

Cell-specific transcription rate

The per-gene constant is replaced by a rate driven by transcription-factor expression through a masked, learnable weight matrix,

$$\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i=\mathrm{softplus}\!\big((W\odot M)\,\mathbf{s}^{\text{TF}}_i+\mathbf{b}\big), \qquad \boldsymbol{\alpha}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{G}_{>0},$$

where $M$ is a binary ENCODE/ChEA TF–target mask, $W$ the learnable weights, and $\mathbf{b}$ a basal bias warm-started from VeloVI and frozen. Unlike static TF-regression methods, $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i$ is optimised end-to-end against the ODE likelihood, with an L1 penalty promoting sparsity.

Supervised residual time head

A two-layer residual MLP on the latent code $\mathbf{z}_i$ produces a calibrated time, $t_i=\sigma(W_3\,\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{h}_1)W_2))$ with $\mathbf{h}_1=\mathrm{ReLU}(W_1\mathbf{z}_i)+W_{\text{skip}}\mathbf{z}_i$, trained by $\mathcal{L}_{\text{time}}=\mathrm{MSE}(t_i,\ \mathrm{sg}(t^{\text{ODE}}_i))$ so it tracks the ODE time without distorting the kinetic parameters.

Velocity–time consistency

A coherent field should point from earlier to later cells. Over index pairs $(i,j)$ sorted so $t_i\le t_j$,

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{vtc}}=\frac{1}{n_p}\sum_{(i,j)} w_{ij}\, \mathrm{ReLU}\!\big(-\!\cos(\mathbf{v}_i,\ \mathbf{s}_j-\mathbf{s}_i)\big),$$

penalising only wrong-pointing velocities. To our knowledge this is the first direct end-to-end pairwise penalty on the angle between velocity and the expression displacement vector.

Velocity–pseudotime blending

After training, scVelo diffusion pseudotime $t^{\text{VPT}}$ on the velocity transition graph is convex-blended with the (sign-aligned) learned time,

$$t^{\star}_i=\beta\,t^{\text{VPT}}_i+(1-\beta)\,t^{\text{head}}_i,\qquad \beta=0.7,$$

fusing a globally grounded topology signal with the locally calibrated time. The blend is a post-hoc step that does not alter the velocity field.

Across the five datasets, TARVI is on par with the strongest baseline on the directional metrics and clearly leads on every temporal metric, while also achieving the best mean kinetic fit among methods that report it.

Qualitative case study: pancreas

On the pancreatic endocrinogenesis benchmark (Ductal → Ngn3 low/high → Alpha/Beta/Delta/Epsilon), TARVI produces clean directional flow from Ductal through the Ngn3 intermediates to all four mature populations, and a smooth $0\!\to\!1$ latent time (Ductal $\approx 0$, mature $\approx 1$). VeloVI recovers the same topology but with spurious streamlines near Ductal and an uncalibrated time range; scVelo dynamical shows circular artefacts at the kinetic switch; TFvelo’s time gradient is reversed (mature earlier than progenitor), mirroring its RootAcc collapse to 0.531; and Velocyto is correctly oriented but with weaker cluster separation. The full streamline and latent-time panels are in the paper.

The components act on different axes. The blend supplies the pseudotime ordering ($\Delta_{\text{VPT}}$ PT-Spear $=0.747-0.320=0.427$) and leaves the velocity field unchanged; its effect is largely model-agnostic, lifting the raw VeloVI graph from 0.331 to 0.714. The TF rate sharpens the velocity field (ICCoH $+0.083$, VelConf $+0.025$, CBDir $+0.022$) with a modest kinetic-fit gain, and the VTC loss improves temporal ordering and root identification (RootAcc $+0.115$). The blend weight $\beta=0.7$ sits between the ablation endpoints ($\beta=0$ ↔ TARVI−VPT; $\beta=1$ ↔ VeloVI+VPT) and exceeds both, confirming the two temporal signals are complementary.

Reproducibility & Scope

All results use a fixed random seed and a deterministic preprocessing pipeline, and we report single-run point estimates. Run-to-run drift on the aggregate means is $\le 0.01$, which is the right scale for reading small differences: gaps on that order (the directional metrics) are ties, whereas the pseudotime contributions are an order of magnitude larger.

A few limitations bound these conclusions. We did not run a multi-seed study, so we report no confidence intervals or paired significance tests. The TF prior is rebuilt per dataset from the (incomplete, tissue-context-dependent) ENCODE/ChEA databases; genes lacking reliable TF edges fall back to VeloVI-like behaviour, so outside the ENCODE/ChEA scope we expect TARVI to approach the no-TF regime rather than to fail. Finally, VeloTrace — a concurrent method targeting the same gap — has no public code release that would allow a faithful re-implementation under our protocol, so it could not be included among the baselines.
