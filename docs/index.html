Abstract

Reconstructing cell-fate trajectories from single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) is fundamental to understanding development and disease. RNA velocity infers cellular dynamics from unspliced-to-spliced mRNA ratios, but existing methods share three key limitations: transcription rates are static gene-level constants that ignore cell-state regulation; latent cell time is weakly supervised and poorly calibrated; and locally inferred velocity vectors are often inconsistent with global trajectories, producing reversed or discontinuous flows. We present TARVI, a deep probabilistic framework that extends the VeloVI variational autoencoder to address all three limitations, through cell-specific transcription rates driven by transcription factor–target regulatory priors (ChEA/ENCODE), a supervised residual latent-time head, novel velocity–time consistency and temporal-smoothness losses, and a velocity–pseudotime blending step. Evaluated on 5 datasets against 4 baselines with 8 metrics, TARVI matches the strongest baseline on cross-boundary direction accuracy (CBDir: 0.933 vs. VeloVI’s 0.931) while improving Spearman pseudotime correlation by 126% over VeloVI (0.747 vs. 0.331), recovering coherent directional flows and smooth developmental gradients where competing methods fail.

Introduction

Reconstructing cell-fate trajectories from a static snapshot of transcriptomes is a central problem in single-cell biology: cells are destroyed during measurement, so the developmental state of every cell must be inferred from expression alone. RNA velocity reframes this as a local extrapolation problem by jointly counting spliced and unspliced mRNA per gene, yielding a vector field over the cell embedding whose streamlines approximate developmental trajectories without experimental time labels. Its biophysical basis is a system of linear ODEs per gene $g$,

$$\frac{du_g}{dt}=\alpha_g(t)-\beta_g u_g,\qquad \frac{ds_g}{dt}=\beta_g u_g-\gamma_g s_g,\qquad v_g=\frac{ds_g}{dt}=\beta_g u_g-\gamma_g s_g,$$

with transcription rate $\alpha_g$, splicing rate $\beta_g$, and degradation rate $\gamma_g$. At steady state the on-state satisfies $u_g/s_g=\gamma_g/\beta_g$; deviations indicate active induction or repression.

Despite a decade of progress, contemporary estimators still suffer from three structural limitations:

  1. the per-gene transcription rate $\alpha_g$ is a static scalar, ignoring cell-state-dependent regulation by transcription-factor (TF) networks;
  2. latent cell time, recovered as a by-product of mixture-state assignment, is weakly supervised and uncalibrated against developmental ordering;
  3. locally inferred velocity vectors and globally observed cell trajectories are estimated independently, leading to reversed flows and instability under count splitting.

Contributions. TARVI explicitly uses TF expression as a mechanistic input to modulate transcription rates, addressing all three gaps jointly:

  • a masked TF$\to$gene weight matrix (ChEA/ENCODE priors) producing cell-specific $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i$, jointly optimised end-to-end against the ODE likelihood;
  • a residual latent-time head supervised by a stop-gradient ODE-time signal, with a graph-Laplacian temporal-smoothness regulariser, plus a velocity–time consistency loss that directly penalises the angle between each cell’s velocity and its displacement toward later cells;
  • a velocity–pseudotime blending step that fuses the supervised time head with velocity-graph diffusion pseudotime for a globally grounded temporal coordinate;
  • evaluation on five biologically diverse datasets against four baselines with eight metrics, where TARVI matches the strongest baseline on cross-boundary direction accuracy while substantially improving pseudotime calibration.

Related Work

Velocyto pioneered RNA velocity by fitting a steady-state slope $\gamma_g/\beta_g$ via linear regression on the $u$–$s$ phase portrait, assuming a single global kinetic regime per gene. scVelo resolved this by solving the full two-state (induction/repression) ODE system analytically and inferring cell times via Expectation–Maximization; however $\alpha_g$ remains a static gene-level constant and the per-gene EM fits scale poorly. VeloVI replaced the EM procedure with a variational autoencoder, enabling scalable amortised inference with uncertainty quantification across four kinetic states, but its transcription rates are still single learnable scalars with no regulatory input and its latent time is poorly calibrated. TFvelo addressed the regulatory gap by modelling $\alpha_g$ as a static linear function of TF expression, improving interpretability, but it is not generative, provides no uncertainty, and produces no latent embedding.

DeepVelo and scTour moved beyond closed-form ODEs by learning continuous dynamics with neural ODEs, and the recent VeloTrace jointly optimises velocity and trajectory with neural ODEs to close the velocity–trajectory gap. On the evaluation side, large-scale benchmarks have standardised community metrics (e.g. CBDir, ICCoH), and recent work identifies instabilities such as reversed velocity flows, motivating a supervised, calibrated time head. TARVI targets these limitations within a single probabilistic generative framework: cell-specific TF-regulated $\alpha_g$, a supervised residual time head, and a velocity–pseudotime blending step.

Method

TARVI builds on the VeloVI four-state generative backbone (induction, induction-steady, repression, repression-steady) with an amortised variational encoder, and adds four components that target the limitations above.

1 · TF-regulated rate

A masked, learnable TF→gene matrix turns the static $\alpha_g$ into a cell-specific $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i$ from ChEA/ENCODE priors.

2 · Supervised time head

A residual MLP on the latent code yields a calibrated $t_i\in[0,1]$, supervised by a stop-gradient ODE-time signal.

3 · Consistency losses

A velocity–time hinge plus a graph-Laplacian temporal-smoothness term keep the field temporally coherent.

4 · VPT blending

Diffusion pseudotime on the velocity graph is blended with the learned time for a globally grounded coordinate.

Preprocessing

Standard preprocessing uses scv.pp.moments after QC, normalisation ($10^4$ counts/cell), log1p, and top-2000 highly-variable-gene selection (Scanpy/scVelo). TARVI additionally applies (a) per-gene MinMax scaling of Ms/Mu to $[0,1]$, and (b) an $r^2$-based gene filter retaining only genes with positive scVelo $r^2$ and $\gamma>0$.

Generative model

For a cell $i$ with scaled spliced/unspliced vectors $\mathbf{s}_i,\mathbf{u}_i\in\mathbb{R}^G$:

$$\mathbf{z}_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_d),\quad (\boldsymbol{\rho}_i,\boldsymbol{\tau}_i,\boldsymbol{\pi}_i)=f_\theta^{\text{dec}}(\mathbf{z}_i),\quad \boldsymbol{\alpha}_i=\mathrm{softplus}\!\big((W\odot M)\,\mathbf{s}^{\text{TF}}_i+\mathbf{b}\big),$$ $$(\mathbf{s}_i,\mathbf{u}_i)\sim\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{4}\pi_{i,g,k}\, \mathcal{N}\!\big(\mu_{i,g,k},\sigma_{g,k}^2\big).$$

The encoder $q_\phi$ maps $[\log(\mathbf{s}+0.01)\,\|\,\log(\mathbf{u}+0.01)]$ to $\mathbf{z}_i\in\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d=10$); the decoder produces induction/repression progress $\boldsymbol{\rho}_i,\boldsymbol{\tau}_i\in[0,1]^G$ and mixture weights $\boldsymbol{\pi}_i\in\Delta^3$.

Four-state kinetics (closed-form ODE solutions)

The induction branch starts from $u{=}s{=}0$ and evolves over $t_{\text{ind}}=t_s\rho_{i,g}$; the repression branch starts from the induction endpoint $(u_0,s_0)$ and evolves over $t_{\text{rep}}=(t_{\max}-t_s)\tau_{i,g}$:

$$u_{\text{ind}}(t)=\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta}\big(1-e^{-\beta t}\big),\quad s_{\text{ind}}(t)=\tfrac{\alpha}{\gamma}\big(1-e^{-\gamma t}\big) +\tfrac{\alpha}{\gamma-\beta}\big(e^{-\gamma t}-e^{-\beta t}\big),$$ $$u_{\text{rep}}(t)=u_0\,e^{-\beta t},\quad s_{\text{rep}}(t)=s_0\,e^{-\gamma t}-\tfrac{\beta u_0}{\gamma-\beta}\big(e^{-\gamma t}-e^{-\beta t}\big).$$

The induction-steady and repression-steady means are $(\alpha/\beta,\alpha/\gamma)$ and $(0,0)$; all rates are softplus-constrained and clamped to $[0,50]$, with learnable $t_s$ and $t_{\max}=20$. With cell-specific $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i$ the same closed forms hold, $\alpha$ broadcast across cells.

TF-regulated transcription rate

A binary mask $M\in\{0,1\}^{G\times K}$ is derived from ENCODE ChIP-seq peak annotations and ChEA 2016 TF–target associations, retaining the top $K\leq99$ TFs per gene; weights are warm-started as $W^{(0)}_{gk}=0.1\,\rho_S(\mathbf{s}_g,\mathbf{s}_k)$.

$$\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i=\mathrm{softplus}\!\big((W\odot M)\,\mathbf{s}^{\text{TF}}_i+\mathbf{b}\big), \qquad \boldsymbol{\alpha}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{G}_{>0}.$$

The bias $\mathbf{b}$ is warm-started from VeloVI’s basal $\alpha_{\text{unconstr}}$ (frozen), so the TF module adds an incremental cell-specific signal; an L1 penalty $\lambda_{\text{L1}}\lVert W\rVert_1/(GK)$ promotes sparsity. Unlike TFvelo, $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i$ is jointly optimised end-to-end against the ODE likelihood. ChEA/ENCODE cover human and mouse, within which the five benchmarks fall.

Residual latent-time head

VeloVI’s cell time $t^{\text{ODE}}_i=\mathbb{E}_{k\sim\boldsymbol{\pi}_i} [t_k(\boldsymbol{\rho}_i,\boldsymbol{\tau}_i,t_s)]$ is discontinuous at state boundaries and weakly supervised. TARVI adds a two-layer residual MLP on $\mathbf{z}_i$,

$$\mathbf{h}_1=\mathrm{ReLU}(W_1\mathbf{z}_i)+W_{\text{skip}}\mathbf{z}_i,\quad \mathbf{h}_2=\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{h}_1)W_2),\quad t_i=\sigma(W_3\mathbf{h}_2)\in[0,1],$$

with skip connection to stabilise the high-KL phase, supervised by $\mathcal{L}_{\text{time}}=\mathrm{MSE}(t_i,\,\mathrm{sg}(t^{\text{ODE}}_i))$ where $\mathrm{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator.

Training losses

The total objective is

$$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}+w_{\text{kl}}\,\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf{z}) +\mathrm{KL}_{\text{cpu}}(\boldsymbol{\pi}) +\lambda_{\text{time}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{time}}+\lambda_{\text{ode}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ode-res}} +\lambda_{\text{vtc}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{vtc}}+\lambda_{\text{ts}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ts}} +\lambda_{\text{L1}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{L1}}.$$

ODE-residual loss (scVelo-inspired). Given $t_i$, the ODE predictions are re-evaluated through a soft gate $g_{ig}=\sigma(5(t_{s,g}-t_i\,t_{\max}))$ that blends the induction and repression branches:

$$\hat{u}_{ig}=g_{ig}u_{\text{ind}}(t_i)+(1-g_{ig})u_{\text{rep}}(t_i),\quad \hat{s}_{ig}=g_{ig}s_{\text{ind}}(t_i)+(1-g_{ig})s_{\text{rep}}(t_i),$$ $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ode-res}}=\tfrac12\!\left[\overline{\big((\hat{s}-s)/\bar{s}\big)^2} +\overline{\big((\hat{u}-u)/\bar{u}\big)^2}\right].$$

Velocity–time consistency loss. For $n_p=\min(B/2,128)$ random pairs $(i,j)$ per minibatch sorted so $t_i\le t_j$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{vtc}}=\frac{1}{n_p}\sum_{(i,j)}w_{ij}\, \mathrm{ReLU}\!\big(-\!\cos(\mathbf{v}_i,\mathbf{s}_j-\mathbf{s}_i)\big),\quad w_{ij}=\mathrm{clip}\!\big(\tfrac{t_j-t_i}{\overline{\Delta t}},0,3\big).$$

The hinge penalises only wrong-pointing velocities. Temporal smoothness is a graph-Laplacian regulariser over in-batch $k$NN pairs in latent space, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ts}}=\frac{1}{Nk}\sum_i\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}^z_i}(t_i-t_j)^2$.

Default weights: $w_{\text{kl}}{=}1.0$, $\eta_{\text{Dir}}{=}0.25$, $\lambda_{\text{time}}{=}1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{ode}}{=}1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{vtc}}{=}0.2$, $\lambda_{\text{ts}}{=}0.1$, $\lambda_{\text{L1}}{=}0.01$.

Velocity–pseudotime blending

After training, scVelo diffusion pseudotime is run on the velocity transition graph to obtain $t^{\text{VPT}}_i\in[0,1]$. The learned time is sign-aligned to $t^{\text{VPT}}$ by Spearman correlation ($t^{\text{head}}\leftarrow 1-t^{\text{head}}$ if $\rho_S(t^{\text{head}},t^{\text{VPT}})<0$) and convex-blended:

$$t^{\star}_i=\beta\,t^{\text{VPT}}_i+(1-\beta)\,t^{\text{head}}_i,\qquad \beta=0.7.$$

$\beta=0.7$ was selected once by grid search over $\{0,0.2,\dots,1.0\}$ on the pooled cross-dataset mean and held fixed for all five datasets; performance is stable across $\beta\in[0.5,0.9]$.

Training procedure

TARVI is trained with AdamW ($\eta_0=10^{-2}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$, gradient clip norm 10) for up to 500 epochs with early stopping on a 10% validation holdout (batch 256, or $\max(32,N/4)$ for small datasets). The VeloVI KL weight ramp is preserved; all auxiliary losses are active from epoch 0.

Results

Across the five datasets, TARVI is on par with the strongest baseline on the directional metrics and clearly leads on every temporal metric, while also achieving the best mean kinetic fit among methods that report it.

Qualitative case study: pancreas

On the pancreatic endocrinogenesis benchmark (Ductal → Ngn3 low/high → Alpha/Beta/Delta/Epsilon), TARVI produces clean directional flow from Ductal through the Ngn3 intermediates to all four mature populations, and a smooth $0\!\to\!1$ latent time (Ductal $\approx 0$, mature $\approx 1$). VeloVI recovers the same topology but with spurious streamlines near Ductal and an uncalibrated time range (${\sim}[2.4,3.4]$); scVelo dynamical shows circular artefacts at the kinetic switch; TFvelo’s time gradient is reversed (mature earlier than progenitor), mirroring its RootAcc collapse to 0.531; and Velocyto is correctly oriented but with weaker cluster separation. The full streamline and latent-time panels are in the paper.

Component Ablation

To attribute performance to each component we ablate the TF rate, the velocity–time consistency loss (VTC), and the velocity-pseudotime blend (VPT) across all five datasets, and also apply the blend to the unmodified VeloVI graph. We write $\Delta_c\,m=m(\text{TARVI})-m(\text{TARVI}\!-\!c)$ for the marginal contribution of component $c$, and $\delta_{\text{VPT}}\,m=m(\text{VeloVI}\!+\!\text{VPT})-m(\text{VeloVI})$ for the blend on the raw VeloVI graph. It is largely model-agnostic: on the raw VeloVI graph $\delta_{\text{VPT}}(\text{PT-Spear})=0.383$; the residual $+0.033$ is what TARVI’s own velocity field adds.
  • The TF rate sharpens the velocity field: $\Delta_{\text{TF}}$ is positive on ICCoH ($+0.083$), VelConf ($+0.025$), CBDir ($+0.022$) and Gene-R²spl ($+0.018$), but not on pseudotime — a biologically grounded regulariser that improves field consistency more than $R^2$.

The velocity-graph pseudotime itself is built from a transition kernel $\pi_{ij}\propto\exp(\cos(\mathbf{v}_i,\mathbf{s}_j-\mathbf{s}_i)/\sigma)$ over $k$NN neighbours, followed by diffusion pseudotime from a root cell — a post-hoc step that does not modify the velocity field.

Reproducibility & Scope

All results use a fixed random seed and a deterministic preprocessing pipeline, and we report single-run point estimates. Run-to-run drift on the aggregate means is $\le 0.01$, which is the right scale for reading small differences: gaps on that order (the directional metrics) are ties, whereas the pseudotime contributions are an order of magnitude larger.

A few limitations bound these conclusions. We did not run a multi-seed study, so we report no confidence intervals or paired significance tests. The TF prior is rebuilt per dataset from the (incomplete, tissue-context-dependent) ENCODE/ChEA databases; genes lacking reliable TF edges fall back to VeloVI-like behaviour, so outside the ENCODE/ChEA scope we expect TARVI to approach the no-TF regime rather than to fail. RootAcc is brittle on multifurcating topologies (bone marrow); macrostate-based terminal detection is a clear next step. Finally, VeloTrace — a concurrent method targeting the same gap — has no public code release that would allow a faithful re-implementation under our protocol, so it could not be included among the baselines.

Conclusion

TARVI is a principled, modular deep generative model for RNA velocity that integrates transcription-factor regulation, a supervised residual time head, ODE-aligned auxiliary losses, and velocity–pseudotime blending into a single variational framework. Across five biologically diverse datasets and four baseline methods, it matches the strongest baseline on cross-boundary direction accuracy and leads on pseudotime quality, with a 126% improvement in Spearman pseudotime correlation over VeloVI. The implementation, benchmark harness, and evaluation suite are available in the repository.
